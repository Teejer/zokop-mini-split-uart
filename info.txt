Here’s how to wire a 74AHCT125 as a 3.3V‑to‑5V UART level shifter for your TX line. This chip is much more robust than the BSS138 module and should fix the problem.

74AHCT125 Pinout
The 74AHCT125 has four independent buffers with active‑low enables. You only need one for TX, but the chip is cheap and you can leave the other inputs grounded.

Pins (for a DIP‑14 or SOIC‑14):

┌───┬───┐
   1OE  │1  │14 │ VCC
    1A  │2  │13 │ 4OE
    1Y  │3  │12 │ 4A
    2Y  │4  │11 │ 4Y
    2A  │5  │10 │ 3OE
    2OE │6  │ 9 │ 3A
   GND  │7  │ 8 │ 3Y
        └───┴───┘
1A (pin 2) = input (3.3V from ESP32)
1Y (pin 3) = output (5V to AC board)
1OE (pin 1) = output enable – tie to GND (always enabled)
VCC (pin 14) = 5V
GND (pin 7) = common ground
Wiring for your TX line
Connect:

74AHCT125 Pin	Connect to
Pin 14 (VCC)	5V (yellow wire from AC harness, or a 5V source)
Pin 7 (GND)	GND (white wire from AC harness + ESP32 GND)
Pin 1 (1OE)	GND (to enable buffer)
Pin 2 (1A)	ESP32 GPIO21 (UART TX) – 3.3V signal
Pin 3 (1Y)	Black wire (AC board RX) – now 5V signal
Pin 4, 5, 6 (2Y, 2A, 2OE)	Leave floating or tie unused inputs to GND
Pins 8–13	Leave floating or tie unused inputs to GND
No other connections needed. The 74AHCT125 output is a strong 5V TTL signal, well within the AC board’s UART specs.

What about RX (AC → ESP32)?
Option 1: Keep your existing level shifter for RX.
If you’re still receiving broadcasts fine through the BSS138 module, you can leave it for RX and just use the 74AHCT125 for TX. This is simplest.

Option 2: Use a voltage divider for RX (no level shifter).
Connect the red wire (AC TX) directly to a resistor divider:

AC red (5V) --- 1kΩ ---+--- 2kΩ --- GND
                        |
                     ESP32 GPIO20 (RX)
The 2kΩ to GND goes to ground; the junction gives about 3.3V. Use resistors between 1k–10k; this works fine at 115200 baud.

Important notes
Do not tie 1OE to VCC – that disables the output.
Common ground is mandatory: ESP32 GND, 74AHCT125 GND, AC GND all tied together.
The 74AHCT125 is unidirectional, so you can’t use it for both TX and RX. That’s why we keep the level shifter for RX or use a divider.
The AC board’s black wire idle voltage is ~5V; the 74AHCT125 output will also be ~5V high when idle, so level matching is correct.
Testing after wiring
Reconnect everything.
In ESPHome, send power_on.
If the AC turns on, you’re done.
You can also watch the logs – you should now see a 0x23 ACK or a state frame with 00 01 01 after your command.

Usage examples
List all supported named commands:

python3 ac_control.py --list
Send power on:

python3 ac_control.py --port /dev/ttyUSB0 --command power_on
Send a raw captured frame exactly as-is:

python3 ac_control.py --port /dev/ttyUSB0 \
  --hex "a5 01 01 21 09 00 00 12 be b6 0a 0a 00 01 01 00 13 00"
Send a custom single-DP command, e.g. horizontal louver left flow (0x0E = 0x02):

python3 ac_control.py --port /dev/ttyUSB0 \
  --hex "$(python3 - <<'PY'
from ac_control import make_state_frame, simple_dp
print(make_state_frame(simple_dp(0x0e, 0x02), seq=0x50).hex(' ').upper())
PY
)"
Or simply add your own custom payload in the script and run:

python3 ac_control.py --port /dev/ttyUSB0 --command louver_h_left_flow
Important wiring note
In your capture, /dev/ttyUSB0 was labeled RX (module → board), meaning that line carries data from the WiFi module to the AC board.

To send commands with this script, connect your USB-UART adapter’s TX to that same module→board signal line, and connect a common ground. Do not power the AC board from the USB adapter.

A few things I noticed:

The “LED display” capture you posted was identical to the fan-speed capture, so I did not include a standalone display_off / display_on command.
The mode payloads I used include several extra DPs from your capture; they should reproduce the exact mode changes you recorded.
If you want to experiment with minimal commands, use simple_dp(0x12, mode) for mode only, but I haven’t confirmed whether the board accepts a partial DP update. The full captured payloads are the safest.
