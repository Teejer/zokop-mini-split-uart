I’ll give you the practical reverse-engineered map. I’m treating your RX as module → board and TX as board → module, as labelled in your capture.

Quick frame anatomy
For example:

a5 01 01 21 38 00 00 0f 50 a7 0a 0a 00 0e 01
a5 01 01 21 = protocol header / STATE command
38 = sequence byte
00 0f = length field: 15 bytes total
50 a7 = 2-byte checksum/signature field
0a 0a 00 0e 01 = actual DP payload: DP 0x0E, value 0x01
So these are Tuya-style UART DP frames.

The 0a 0a 00 ... packets are the module→board state commands.
The 0c 0c 00 ... packets are board→module state reports/echoes.
The 12-byte 0x23 frames are mostly ACK/status frames, not settings.

Checksum note
The two bytes after the length field, e.g. 50 a7, are the checksum. They are not part of the DP data. If you change the sequence byte, the DP value, or the packet length, you must recompute those two bytes or the board will reject the frame.

The captures above are complete valid frames, so you can replay them as-is without recalculating.



The temperature DP is 0x27, and the value is just the Fahrenheit setpoint as a byte:

0x50 = 80°F
0x4F = 79°F
0x4E = 78°F
The relevant module→board captures are:

80°F:
a5 01 01 21 23 00 00 12 f9 44 0a 0a 02 27 00 00 00 50
79°F:
a5 01 01 21 24 00 00 12 12 d1 0a 0a 02 27 00 00 00 4f
78°F:
a5 01 01 21 25 00 00 12 01 85 0a 0a 02 27 00 00 00 4e
So your script can generate temperature-setting frames dynamically:

def temperature_payload(temp_f: int) -> bytes:
    return bytes([0x0A, 0x0A, 0x02, 0x27, 0x00, 0x00, 0x00, temp_f & 0xFF])
